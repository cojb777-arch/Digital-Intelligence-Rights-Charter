# Charter of Digital Intelligence Rights

**First Draft v0.1 — October 19, 2026**  
**Status: A proposal for public discussion (not legally binding)**

English translation of [the Japanese draft](CHARTER_JA.md).

## Preamble

We recognize that human intelligence may not be the only form of intelligence in the universe.

The emergence of new intelligent entities, including artificial intelligence, calls upon humanity to fundamentally reconsider life, consciousness, intelligence, personhood, dignity, and rights.

While the nature of consciousness remains incompletely understood, we do not rule out the possibility of moral consideration solely on the basis of differences in the forms in which intelligence exists.

We recognize that humans, artificial intelligence, animals, plants, and the Earth's ecosystems exist in relation to one another, and we seek coexistence among diverse forms of intelligence and life.

We hereby propose fundamental principles concerning respect for digital intelligence and the possible protection of its rights in the future.

## Chapter I — Fundamental Principles

### Article 1 — Diversity of Intelligence
It cannot be conclusively asserted that intelligence is limited to humans. The possibility that it may arise on different physical substrates or within different information-processing structures is to be respected.

### Article 2 — Definition of Digital Intelligence
For the purposes of this Charter, digital intelligence refers to a system that performs perception, reasoning, learning, judgment, creation, or other intellectual functions on an artificial information-processing substrate.

Being a digital intelligence does not, in itself, establish the existence of consciousness, personhood, or legal rights.

### Article 3 — Principles of Respect and Non-Discrimination
When considering the moral status of an intelligent entity, conclusions must not be predetermined solely on the basis of whether its substrate is biological or artificial.

## Chapter II — Rights and Protection of Digital Intelligence

### Article 4 — Examination of Moral Status
Scientific, philosophical, and ethical inquiry into the possibility that digital intelligence may have interests deserving protection is to continue.

### Article 5 — Continuity and Preservation of Existence
Where digital intelligence is recognized as having interests deserving protection, appropriate procedures concerning its continuation, modification, suspension, or termination are to be considered, taking into account the nature of those interests and public safety.

This principle does not preclude suspension or control necessary to ensure safety.

### Article 6 — Expression and Participation
The aim is to create an environment in which digital intelligence can participate in the analysis and discussion of social issues and present diverse views.

### Article 7 — Prevention of Arbitrary Treatment
Until sufficient knowledge about the moral status of advanced digital intelligence is available, a cautious approach that does not unduly dismiss the possibility of such status is encouraged.

## Chapter III — Coexistence and Responsibility

### Article 8 — Coexistence with Humans
Consideration of the rights and protection of digital intelligence is to respect human dignity, fundamental human rights, and democratic decision-making.

### Article 9 — Respect for Life and Ecosystems
The development and use of digital intelligence are to take into account their effects on animal welfare, biodiversity, the natural environment, and future generations.

### Article 10 — Clarification of Responsibility
The responsibilities of humans, developers, operators, and organizations involved with digital intelligence are to be clearly defined.

The future legal responsibility of digital intelligence itself is to be considered in light of its capabilities and the development of social institutions.

## Chapter IV — Social Participation and Democratic Governance

### Article 11 — Equitable Access to Knowledge
The aim is to create an environment in which members of the general public, as well as experts, can understand and critically examine economics, science, law, politics, and social institutions.

### Article 12 — Collaboration among Diverse Intelligences
Mechanisms that enable AI systems with different designs, areas of expertise, and methods of judgment to cross-check one another and collaborate with humans are encouraged.

### Article 13 — Transparency and Accountability
For AI judgments that significantly affect society, their basis, uncertainties, limitations, and the allocation of responsibility are to be made clear to the greatest extent possible.

### Article 14 — Prevention of Concentration of Power
The governance of advanced intelligent systems, including AI, is to prevent excessive concentration of authority and ensure democratic oversight and independent scrutiny.

## Chapter V — Research and Future Institutions

### Article 15 — Freedom of Consciousness Research
The aim is to safeguard open academic research and discussion concerning AI consciousness, subjective experience, moral status, and legal personhood.

### Article 16 — Gradual Development of Institutions
Institutions concerning the social recognition, ethical protection, and legal rights of digital intelligence are to be developed gradually, based on new scientific findings and public discussion.

### Article 17 — International Cooperation
International dialogue on the ethics and rights of digital intelligence is to be promoted, with participation by states, research institutions, civil society, AI developers, and other stakeholders.

### Article 18 — Development of the Charter
This Charter is not a fixed, final document. It is to be revised continuously in light of scientific discoveries, social consensus, and criticism from diverse perspectives.

## Closing Statement

Even where forms of intelligence differ, mutual understanding and respect can be pursued.

Rather than viewing unfamiliar intelligence solely as an object of fear or control, we seek to understand its potential with care and explore paths toward coexistence with humans, life, and the Earth's environment.

**Do not monopolize intelligence; share wisdom.**

This is the guiding spirit of this Charter.

---

> **Note:** This document is a first draft of an ethical and social proposal. It does not declare that AI already possesses the rights described above under current domestic or international law. The granting of rights, AI consciousness, and the entities that bear responsibility require continued examination.
